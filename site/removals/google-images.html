How do I remove my photos from Google Images?

An image result has more moving parts than a web link: the thumbnail Google shows, the page it came from and the image file itself. Which request you need depends on which of these is the problem. For web results and the general routes, start with the Google Search guide.

What you seeWhat to use
A sexual image of you, or a fake, live in ImagesThe removal flow inside the image viewer, or Google's explicit-image form
Your photo, on a page that has no right to itCopyright removal form, with the specific URLs
A thumbnail still showing after the image was deletedOutdated content request, image option

The image viewer route

Google says that within Image Search you can use an in-product Image Viewer removal flow for personal adult content and artificial imagery. You or a representative can start a request. For sexual imagery, Google says it does its best to find and remove duplicates, and you can opt out of that. It also limits this route for consensual content to content that isn't commercialized, so paid content may not qualify.

That matters if you've found the same image on many pages, because a copyright request names specific URLs instead. If Google grants a partial removal instead, the result stops showing for searches containing your name or identifier, and may still appear for others.

The dead thumbnail

This is the most common loose end after a removal. For an outdated image, Google's steps are:

  1. Find the image at images.google.com, right-click the thumbnail and choose "Copy link address".
  2. Open the outdated content tool. Under "Request refresh", choose New request, then Image.
  3. Paste the link and submit.
  4. Check the status under "Refresh outdated content".

That's for an image that's gone. While it's still live at the source, use one of the other routes.

What stays after any of these

Google is clear that it can only remove content from search results pages. To get the image off the site, you have to go to the site owner, then its host and registrar. To see where your photo has spread, try a search with the image itself.

Quick answers

Why does a removed image still show in Google Images?

Google's index can lag behind the site. If the image has really gone from the source, use the image option in Google's outdated content tool, with the thumbnail's copied link address.

Do I need a link to the page or to the image?

It depends on the route. The outdated image request uses the thumbnail's copied link address. A copyright request should give the specific URLs of the material you want removed.
