How do I prove the content is mine?

Less than you'd think, at first. A DMCA notice asks you to identify the work and state, under penalty of perjury for your authority to act, that the information is accurate. It doesn't ask you to attach a certificate. Proof comes in when someone asks for it, disputes it, or when you're the one who has to be sure. Whether you own the rights at all is a separate question.

What identifies a work, strongest first

  1. A link to your original post. That's what I ask for, now and then, when filing. It's the simplest way of showing where it came from.
  2. The original file. A higher-quality or longer version than the leak, with its creation details, that nobody else would have. Keep it on your own device.
  3. Upload history. Platform records or emails showing you posted it before the leak appeared.
  4. A shoot's other frames. Photos taken just before and after one that's in dispute. A re-poster rarely has them.
  5. A registration certificate, if you have one. See below.

If your account is gone

Retiring doesn't remove your rights, but it does remove your easiest evidence. If you're closing an account, first save your post list, your earnings or post statements if the platform offers them, and the original files. If it's already closed, check old backups and your email for upload or payout notices from that time.

If you're faceless

You can't rely on a recipient recognizing you, so lean on what only the creator would have: the unposted frames from a set, a longer version of a clip, or your own post showing the same content first. A distinctive background you've used in other posts helps as well.

Registration: stronger, not private

In a US court, a certificate made before or within five years of first publication counts as prima facie evidence that the copyright is valid. Fees are $45 for a single work by one author, or $55 for a group of photographs. You don't need it to send a notice.

Think hard before you do it with adult content. Copyright Office records are open to public inspection, and so are deposits it keeps. If privacy matters to you, talk to a lawyer about what would become visible.

When you can't show it

If you honestly can't tell whether it's yours, leave it out. A notice carries real liability if you knowingly misrepresent something, and I don't file against a link unless I've confirmed it's really yours.

General information, not legal advice. I'm not a lawyer and Abolita is not a law firm. Checked against the linked sources on 6 November 2026.

Quick answers

Do I have to show my face or ID to file a notice?

The statute's notice elements don't ask for a photo of you or for ID. They ask you to identify the work and the copy, give contact details, sign, and make the required statements. A recipient may ask for more.

Is copyright registration needed to prove it's mine?

Not to send a notice. A certificate gives you stronger evidence in a US court, but Copyright Office records and retained deposits are open to public inspection, so weigh that up first.
