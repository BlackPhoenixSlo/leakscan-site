Can I sue over leaked content?

Sometimes, yes, and you don't need to sue to get a takedown. This is a short map of US federal options, so you know what to ask a lawyer. Other countries have their own rules. I'm not a lawyer and Abolita isn't a law firm.

Copyright in federal court

  • Registration first. For a United States work, you can't start the action until the claim is registered or preregistered. If the work was created outside the US, ask a lawyer how this applies.
  • Timing affects what you can recover. Section 412 generally rules out statutory damages and attorney's fees if the infringement began before registration, unless you registered within three months of first publication.
  • What you can claim. Actual damages and the infringer's profits, or statutory damages of $750 to $30,000 per work, up to $150,000 if willful.
  • The deadline. Three years from when the claim accrued.

The Copyright Claims Board

The CCB is an alternative to federal court, with a cap of $30,000 in total and $15,000 per work in statutory damages. You don't need an attorney, and you need to have at least applied for registration. The catch: the other side can opt out, and then you're back to federal court.

Intimate images

15 U.S.C. § 6851 gives a person whose intimate image was disclosed without consent a federal civil claim. It allows actual damages or $150,000 in liquidated damages, costs and fees, and court orders to stop the display, and a court can protect a plaintiff who uses a pseudonym.

There's an important exception for creators: you can't sue under it over "commercial pornographic content", unless that content was produced by force, fraud, misrepresentation or coercion. So content you made to sell may not fit. That's a question for your lawyer.

The real obstacles

  • Who to sue. Uploaders are often anonymous. Section 512(h) lets a copyright owner ask a US court clerk for a subpoena to a provider to identify an alleged infringer, but that only helps if the provider has useful records.
  • Where they are. A judgment against someone abroad, or with no money, may not be worth much.
  • Your name. A lawsuit is a public record unless the court allows otherwise.

Keep your options open

Whatever you decide, keep the leak links, a link to your original post, and the date you found each. Send notices anyway: you can do both. If someone is threatening you, go to a local lawyer or the police first, not to a takedown service.

Quick answers

Do I have to register before I sue?

For a United States work, yes: the statute bars an infringement action until the claim is registered or preregistered. Registering late can also rule out statutory damages and attorney's fees.

Can I sue without a lawyer?

The Copyright Claims Board says you don't need an attorney to file or respond to a claim there. For federal court, talk to a lawyer.
