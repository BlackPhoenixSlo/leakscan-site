Do it yourself, or hire a takedown service?

For a handful of links, do it yourself. It's free, nobody needs a lawyer to send a notice, and these guides have what you need. I sell this service and I'll still tell you that. It's mostly time and repetition, so the question is how much of that you have.

Your situationWhat I'd do
A few links, on platforms with a report formDIY. Pick the guide for each place
One link, on a site that answersDIY, with this template
Images of you, posted without consentStart with the consent routes. They're free and don't depend on a service
Many sites, or the same content keeps coming backA service starts to make sense
You don't want to keep looking at thisHand it to someone. That's a good enough reason
A dispute, a threat, or you're thinking of suingA lawyer, not a takedown service

What DIY actually involves

  • Finding the links, and finding them again every few weeks.
  • Checking each one is really you.
  • Working out who to send it to, and going up to host and registrar when a site ignores you.
  • Filing with search engines separately.
  • Keeping a record of it all.

What a service doesn't change

It can't make a site that ignores every notice answer, so no honest service promises everything comes down. It doesn't change the law either: the notice still has to be accurate, and it's still filed for you, under your authorization. Speed and scale can also cut the other way. Automated tools file at volume and can hit your own profile, a page you licensed or someone who just looks like you, and one wrong notice can get your accounts flagged.

What to ask any service

  • Does a person check each link before it's filed?
  • Whose name goes on the notices, and who ends up seeing it?
  • Do you get a list of every link and its status?
  • What happens to re-uploads, and when you stop paying?
  • Can you see the work before you pay?

Quick answers

Do I need a lawyer to do it myself?

No. Anyone can send a DMCA notice for their own content, as long as it's accurate and made in good faith. If it turns into a dispute or a court case, that's the time for a lawyer.

Is a service worth it for one link?

Usually not. One link on a site that answers notices is a job you can do yourself. A service starts to make sense when there are many sites or constant re-uploads.
