Do watermarks stop leaks?

No. Anyone who can view a picture can copy it, and a watermark doesn't change that. What it can do comes later: it says whose work it is, it can add a legal hook, and it makes your copy easier to recognize. Worth doing if it doesn't spoil your content, but it isn't protection by itself.

What the law says

  • You don't need one to have copyright. Protection starts when the work is created and fixed, and a copyright notice is optional for works published on or after 1 March 1989.
  • A notice has some value. The Copyright Office says it makes people aware copyright is claimed, and on a published work it may stop an infringer arguing they did so innocently. A full notice is ©, the year of first publication and the owner's name.
  • Removing it can be its own violation. Under 17 U.S.C. § 1202, "copyright management information" includes the author's name and the work's title. Intentionally removing it is prohibited where the person knows, or has reasonable grounds to know, it will help an infringement.

That last one is a court-level point, and whether a given mark qualifies is a question for a lawyer. A stage name on a photo may help, but don't count on it until someone qualified has looked.

What it can't do

  • Stop screenshots or screen recordings.
  • Survive a crop if it sits in a corner.
  • Replace a notice. A stripped or absent watermark doesn't stop you filing, because copyright doesn't depend on it.

If you use one, here's what I'd do

This is my opinion rather than a rule:

  • Put it near the subject, not in a corner, where cropping it out costs the thief the picture.
  • Use the handle fans know you by, so a stolen copy still points to you.
  • Keep your original, unmarked files. They're what help you show it's yours if there's a dispute.
  • Keep the same mark on everything. It makes checking a suspect link much quicker.

And a warning: a visible handle also ties the copy to your name, so it can show up when people search for you. Weigh that if you keep your accounts separate.

When I check a link, a mark helps confirm it's yours, but I don't need one. I compare each result with your content before I file anything.

General information, not legal advice. I'm not a lawyer and Abolita is not a law firm. Checked against the linked sources on 6 November 2026.

Quick answers

Can I still file a takedown if there's no watermark?

Yes. Copyright exists from the moment the work is created and fixed, and a DMCA notice doesn't ask for a watermark. A link to your original post usually does the job.

Is it illegal for someone to remove my watermark?

It can be. US law prohibits intentionally removing copyright management information, such as an author's name, when the person knows or has reasonable grounds to know it will help infringement. Whether your mark qualifies is a question for a lawyer.
