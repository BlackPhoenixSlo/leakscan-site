How do I find my own leaks?

Before you file anything, find out how far it's gone. One leak link rarely means one copy, and it's a job you can do yourself in an evening with free tools. None of them sees everything, so use more than one.

1. Search your names properly

Google's search operators make this much sharper. Try each username you've ever used, old ones included:

"yourhandle" leak
"yourhandle" -site:onlyfans.com
site:reddit.com "yourhandle"

Quotes match the exact phrase, site: limits to one site, and a minus sign leaves out a word or site, such as your own profile. Check the Images and Videos tabs too. Those results often differ from the main ones.

2. Search with the image

Google's search with an image starts from a photo instead of a word. Upload or drag in the picture, and the results can include websites with the image or a similar one. Use a non-explicit, recognizable frame. It's good for catching copies that don't use your name, and it may also show lookalikes, so check each result.

3. Set an alert

Google Alerts emails you when new results match a topic. Enter your handle in quotes, open "Show options", then set how often it runs and which sources it uses. An alert only covers what's new and what Google indexes.

What all of these miss

  • Private groups and chats. Nothing that searches the web reaches those.
  • Pages search engines haven't indexed, or have already dropped.
  • Copies with no name attached, unless an image search happens to catch them.

Keep a list, and stay safe

For each link, note the address, the date you found it and where the original is. Then check that each is really you, because lookalikes turn up. Don't download anything from these sites, don't pay or sign in to one that offers to "delete" your content, and never upload or keep anything from when you were under 18.

Under 18, or the images were taken when you were under 18? Don't send them to anyone, me included. Take It Down, run by NCMEC, works from your own device: your file stays there and only a digital fingerprint is shared with participating platforms.

Quick answers

Is there a free way to check for leaks?

Yes. Exact-match searches, Google's search with an image and Google Alerts are free, and so is my scan, which needs only your username.

Will searching my own name make it worse?

I don't know of anything from Google saying your own searches change how a page ranks for others. What can hurt is downloading from these sites or paying someone there, so don't.
