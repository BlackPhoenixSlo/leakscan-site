FAQ

Straight answers.

Is this legal?

Yes. If you created the content, you hold the copyright to it. A DMCA notice, and its equivalents in the EU and elsewhere, is the legal process for asking sites, hosts and Google to remove copies posted without your permission. You sign a one-page authorization so I can file those notices on your behalf.

Will my own accounts be affected?

That's the exact risk I'm here to avoid. Nothing is filed until I've confirmed by hand that a link is really your content on a site you didn't authorize. Your own profiles, pages you licensed and people who just look like you are never filed against.

What do you need from me?

For the free scan: your creator name and your handles. That's it. Before I file anything I need the signed one-page Copyright Enforcement Authorization, and now and then a link to your original post so I can show it's yours. I never need your passwords or logins.

How fast does it work?

It depends on the site. Some act on a notice quickly; others ignore it, and then I go to their host, their registrar and Google. With the one-time clean, everything I find is filed in the first week. Google can keep showing a dead page for a while, which is why I also file "remove outdated content" requests.

What if it comes back?

Re-uploads happen. On the monthly plan I re-scan every week and file new copies as I find them. On the one-time clean, re-uploads are covered for 40 days, and on day 40 you decide whether to continue.

What will you never do?

File anything without your signed authorization. File against a link I haven't checked myself. Ask for your passwords. Promise you 100% removal: some sites ignore every notice, and I'd rather tell you that up front.

Can I cancel? Does anything auto-renew?

The monthly plan can be cancelled anytime. The one-time clean never auto-renews: after 40 days nothing happens unless you say so. And because the scan and the first removal are free, you only pay once you've seen the work.

Is my information safe?

Your details are used only to answer you and to do the work. A copyright notice has to name the rights holder or their agent, and some recipients forward notices to the uploader or publish a copy (Google, for example, sends copies to the Lumen database). Ask me before we start and I'll walk you through what each one sees. This site has no cookies and no tracking.

Do I need to register or create an account?

No. There's no account, no form, no app and no card. You message me on Telegram, WhatsApp or email, and everything happens in that chat.
